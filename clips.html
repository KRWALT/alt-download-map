The History of Race in America

Thirteen moments worth sending

Each one is two or three minutes from Jeffery Robinson's 80-minute talk, with a question to ask afterward. Pick the one that fits the person you're thinking about, and send it to them directly.

Moment 1 · 2 min

Who Controls the Past

A Harvard-trained lawyer asks how he never learned his own country's history.

Then ask: Who decided what history you learned in school?

 Moment 2 · 2 min

The Man on the $20 Bill

A runaway-slave ad, signed by the man on the $20 bill.

Then ask: What do you know about the people on our money?

 Moment 3 · 2 min

The Third Verse

The verse of the national anthem nobody sings.

Then ask: Have you ever heard the third verse of the national anthem?

 Moment 4 · 2 min

Follow the Money

By 1860, enslaved people were worth more than every bank, factory and railroad in America combined.

Then ask: Who got rich from slavery?

 Moment 5 · 2 min

What Lincoln Actually Said

Read Lincoln's own words, then decide.

Then ask: What were you taught about why Lincoln fought the Civil War?

 Moment 6 · 2 min

Who Gets a Monument

1,503 Confederate monuments. Here's what they honor.

Then ask: Why do you think most of these monuments went up decades after the war ended?

 Moment 7 · 2 min

Tulsa, 1921

One of the first air attacks on American soil was on Black homes in Tulsa.

Then ask: When did you first hear about Tulsa?

 Moment 8 · 3 min

Unless You're Black

"We're going to help you buy a home, unless you're Black."

Then ask: How did your family buy its first home?

 Moment 9 · 2 min

The Real Heroes of the Boycott

373 days of walking. Meet the real heroes of the Montgomery bus boycott.

Then ask: How far would you be willing to walk?

 Moment 10 · 2 min

The Dr. King They Don't Quote

The Dr. King quote they leave out of the holiday speeches.

Then ask: Which Dr. King were you taught about?

 Moment 11 · 2 min

Separate but Equal, Then and Now

It took 89 years after the Civil War for a Black child to sit in a white classroom. Schools today are more segregated than in 1954.

Then ask: Who sat next to you in school?

 Moment 12 · 3 min

In Their Own Words

Why did the South secede? The states wrote it down. So did the man who designed the flag.

Then ask: What does the Confederate flag mean to the people you know?

 Moment 13 · 2 min

Carved in Stone

The rebirth of the KKK, the largest bas-relief in the world, and Georgia tax dollars. It was finished in 1972.

Then ask: Who decides which history gets carved in stone?
