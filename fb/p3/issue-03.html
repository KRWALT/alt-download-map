We returned to UPenn in September to work with all first-year students, as we have been doing the past few years.

"Why Do We Keep Bringing That Up?"

Today is the anniversary of the Gettysburg Address. On November 19, 1863, four months after the battle, Abraham Lincoln stood in a brand new cemetery and spoke for about two minutes. He asked the country to finish what those soldiers died for, and to find "a new birth of freedom."

That was 163 years ago, and it's right about where a lot of our conversations start. Somebody you care about says, "That was so long ago. Why do we keep bringing it up?"

Maybe they're tired. Maybe they feel blamed. You won't know until you ask.

Clarify: "When you say we keep bringing it up, what have you been hearing?"

Probe: "What's it like for you when this history comes up?"

Redirect: "Thank you for telling me. There's a short video that changed how I see all this. Would you watch it with me and tell me what you think?"

Most of you have already seen The History of Race in America. For the people in your life who haven't, we cut it into 13 short moments, two or three minutes each. Pick one to send. It's a good thing to have in your pocket going into the holiday season.
